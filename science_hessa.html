For the Grown-Up

What she will learn this week: By Friday she should be able to name the five senses, say which body part goes with each one, describe what each sense tells us, and explain that the brain is the boss that reads all the messages. She will also learn that senses help keep us safe.

Time: Five sessions of about 30 to 40 minutes. Sit next to her for the "See it first!" boxes and the activities, and let her read as much as she can herself.

How to use the "See it first!" boxes: Before reading each page, open Google Images (turn SafeSearch on), type the exact search phrase in the box, and look at the pictures together for a minute or two. Then read the explanation. Talk about what she noticed. Pictures first makes the words easier to understand.

The Big Idea: Your Body Has Helpers

How your body finds out about the world

In Progress

Topic 1: Big Idea + Sight (Eyes) & Pupil Experiment

Day 1

▢ SEE IT FIRST!

Open Google Images and search: five senses for kids
Look at the pictures. Can you point to the body part for each sense? Which one do you use the most?

🔍 Search: five senses for kids ↗

Right now, your body is finding out about the world. You can see these words. You might hear a sound in the room. You can feel the paper under your hand. How does your body know all this?

Your body has special helpers called senses. A sense is a way that your body finds out what is happening around you. Humans have five main senses:

  • Sight: you see with your eyes
  • Hearing: you hear with your ears
  • Smell: you smell with your nose
  • Taste: you taste with your tongue
  • Touch: you feel with your skin

The body part that does the job for each sense is called a sense organ. But the sense organs cannot do everything on their own. They send messages along tiny pathways in your body called nerves. Nerves are a bit like wires that carry messages. The messages travel to your brain. Your brain is the boss. It reads all the messages and tells you what they mean: "That is a red apple." "That is a bird singing." "That stove is hot!"

FUN FACT: Your brain is working out messages from your senses all day long, even when you are asleep. That is why a loud noise or a strange smell can wake you up!
Words to Know
  • sense: a way your body finds out about the world
  • sense organ: a body part that does the job of a sense
  • nerve: a tiny pathway that carries messages to the brain
  • brain: the boss of the body that reads the messages
  • sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch: the five main senses

▢ SEE IT FIRST! Sight: Your Eyes

Open Google Images and search: parts of the human eye diagram for kids
Then search: eye pupil getting bigger and smaller
Or open kids.britannica.com and search for "eye". Find the colored part and the black dot in the middle.

🔍 Parts of the eye diagram ↗ 🔍 Pupil bigger and smaller ↗ 🌐 Britannica Kids: Eye ↗

You see with your eyes. But here is a surprise: your eyes do not make light. You can only see things because light bounces off them and travels into your eyes. That is why it is hard to see in a very dark room. When there is not enough light, there is nothing bouncing into your eyes!

Look at a friend's eye. In the middle is a black circle called the pupil. The pupil is a hole that lets light in. Around it is the colored part, called the iris. The iris can be brown, green, blue, or grey. The iris works like a tiny curtain. In a dark place, it opens the pupil wide to let in more light. In a bright place, it makes the pupil small so too much light does not get in.

Inside your eye, the light lands on a special spot at the back. Nerves carry the message to your brain, and your brain makes the picture. Your eyes are very soft and important, so your body protects them. Eyelids close to keep dust out. Eyelashes catch tiny bits of dirt. Tears wash the eye clean and keep it wet.

🧪 ACTIVITY: Watch a Pupil Change

1. Sit with a grown-up in a dim room. Look at your own eyes in a mirror. Notice how big your pupils are.
2. Have the grown-up switch on the room light while you keep looking in the mirror. Watch your pupils get smaller!
3. Turn the light off again and wait. Do they get bigger?

⚠️ Never shine a torch into anyone's eyes.

Topic 2: Hearing (Ears), Sound Walk & Vibration Experiments

Day 2

▢ SEE IT FIRST! Hearing: Your Ears

Open Google Images and search: parts of the ear diagram for kids
Then search: how sound travels into the ear for kids
Find the part you can see on the outside of your head. What is it called?

🔍 Parts of the ear diagram ↗ 🔍 How sound travels into the ear ↗

You hear with your ears. Every sound is made by something vibrating, which means wiggling back and forth very fast. A guitar string wiggles. A drum skin wiggles. Your voice box wiggles when you talk. These wiggles push the air, and the air carries the wiggles to your ears.

The part of the ear you can see is like a funnel that catches sounds. Inside, the wiggles hit a thin skin called the eardrum. The eardrum wiggles like the top of a little drum. Next, tiny bones and a snail-shaped part inside your head turn the wiggles into messages. Nerves carry the messages to your brain, and your brain says, "That is a dog barking!"

FUN FACT: Loud sounds have big wiggles that can hurt the tiny parts inside your ear. Keep music and headphones low, and cover your ears near very loud noises.

🧪 ACTIVITY: Listen and Feel

Sound walk: Sit very still for two minutes with your eyes closed. Listen carefully. Then write or draw every sound you heard.
Feel the voice: Put your fingers gently on your throat and hum. What do you feel? That is your voice vibrating!
Twang: Stretch a rubber band between your fingers and pluck it. Can you see and hear it wiggle?

Day 3

▢ SEE IT FIRST! Smell: Your Nose

Open Google Images and search: how does the nose smell diagram for kids
Look for the smell nerve. Where does the message go?

🔍 How does the nose smell diagram ↗

You smell with your nose. Everything that has a smell gives off tiny, tiny bits that are too small to see. These bits float in the air. When you breathe in, the bits go up your nose to a special patch high inside. There, nerves pick up the message and send it to your brain. Your brain says, "Yum, that is bread!" or "Yuck, that is rotten!"

Your sense of smell helps you in many ways. It helps you enjoy food and flowers. It can also warn you of danger, like smoke from a fire or milk that has gone bad. Smell and taste are best friends. When you have a cold and your nose is blocked, food can seem to have very little taste.

FUN FACT: A dog's nose is much, much better than ours. Dogs can smell things people cannot smell at all, which is why some dogs are trained to find lost people.

▢ SEE IT FIRST! Taste: Your Tongue

Open Google Images and search: taste buds on tongue for kids
Then search: five basic tastes sweet sour salty bitter umami
Look at the little bumps on the tongue. Those bumps hold your taste buds.

🔍 Taste buds on tongue diagram ↗ 🔍 Five basic tastes diagram ↗

You taste with your tongue. Look at your tongue in the mirror. It is covered with tiny bumps. On the bumps are even tinier taste buds, and you have thousands of them! When food touches the taste buds, they send a message to your brain about what you are tasting.

Your tongue can tell five main tastes: sweet (like honey), sour (like lemon), salty (like chips), bitter (like dark chocolate), and umami, which means savory, like the taste of soup or cheese. Some people think that each taste happens on only one part of the tongue. That is a myth! Every part of your tongue can taste all of them.

Taste works together with smell. Try this: pinch your nose shut, close your eyes, and have a grown-up give you a small piece of apple and a small piece of raw potato. Can you tell which is which? Now let go of your nose and try again. Your nose helps your tongue a lot!

⚠️ SAFETY FIRST

Check for food allergies first. Only taste what a grown-up gives you. Never taste anything you find.

Topic 4: Touch (Skin). Mystery Bag. "Which Sense?" and "Senses Keep Us Safe"

Day 4

▢ SEE IT FIRST! Touch: Your Skin

Open Google Images and search: layers of skin diagram for kids
Then search: braille alphabet for kids to see how some people read with their fingertips.

🔍 Layers of skin diagram ↗ 🔍 Braille alphabet for kids ↗

You feel things with your skin. Skin covers your whole body, and it is your body's biggest organ! Inside your skin are millions of tiny sensors. They pick up messages about what things feel like: hot or cold, soft or rough, smooth or bumpy, light touches and hard pressure. The sensors send the messages along nerves to the brain.

Some parts of your skin are extra good at feeling. Your fingertips and lips have lots of sensors, so they can feel very small details. Skin also feels pain. Pain is not fun, but it is very useful. It tells you to pull your hand away from something hot or sharp before you get badly hurt.

FUN FACT: Some people cannot see, so they read with their fingertips using a special code of raised dots called braille. Their sense of touch is very strong!

▢ SEE IT FIRST! Senses Keep Us Safe

Open Google Images and search: how the senses keep us safe for kids
Look at the pictures. Which senses are being used to stay safe in each one?

🔍 How the senses keep us safe diagram ↗

Your senses are like a team of guards. They watch for danger all day long. Eyes spot a car coming. Ears hear a horn or an alarm. Your nose smells smoke. Your tongue tastes when food has gone bad. Your skin feels that a pan is too hot. The sense organs send the message, the nerves carry it, and the brain decides what to do, sometimes in a split second!
