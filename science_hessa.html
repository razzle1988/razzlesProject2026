Page 3: Big Idea — Your Senses & The Brain

Day 1

🔍 Search: five senses for kids ↗

Right now, your body is finding out about the world[cite: 53]. You can see these words[cite: 53]. You might hear a sound in the room[cite: 53]. You can feel the paper under your hand[cite: 53]. How does your body know all this?[cite: 53]

Your body has special helpers called senses[cite: 53]. A sense is a way that your body finds out what is happening around you[cite: 53]. Humans have five main senses:[cite: 53]

  • Sight: you see with your eyes[cite: 53]
  • Hearing: you hear with your ears[cite: 53]
  • Smell: you smell with your nose[cite: 53]
  • Taste: you taste with your tongue[cite: 53]
  • Touch: you feel with your skin[cite: 53]

The body part that does the job for each sense is called a sense organ[cite: 53]. But the sense organs cannot do everything on their own[cite: 53]. They send messages along tiny pathways in your body called nerves[cite: 53]. Nerves are a bit like wires that carry messages[cite: 53]. The messages travel to your brain[cite: 53]. Your brain is the boss[cite: 53]. It reads all the messages and tells you what they mean: "That is a red apple." "That is a bird singing." "That stove is hot!"[cite: 53]

💡 FUN FACT: Your brain is working out messages from your senses all day long, even when you are asleep[cite: 53]. That is why a loud noise or a strange smell can wake you up![cite: 53]
Words to Know
  • sense: a way your body finds out about the world[cite: 53]
  • sense organ: a body part that does the job of a sense[cite: 53]
  • nerve: a tiny pathway that carries messages to the brain[cite: 53]
  • brain: the boss of the body that reads the messages[cite: 53]
  • sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch: the five main senses[cite: 53]

Exploring the World Through Sight
The Human Eye & Pupil

You see with your eyes[cite: 54]. But here is a surprise: your eyes do not make light[cite: 54]. You can only see things because light bounces off them and travels into your eyes[cite: 54]. That is why it is hard to see in a very dark room[cite: 54]. When there is not enough light, there is nothing bouncing into your eyes![cite: 54]

Look at a friend's eye[cite: 54]. In the middle is a black circle called the pupil[cite: 54]. The pupil is a hole that lets light in[cite: 54]. Around it is the colored part, called the iris[cite: 54]. The iris can be brown, green, blue, or grey[cite: 54]. The iris works like a tiny curtain[cite: 54]. In a dark place, it opens the pupil wide to let in more light[cite: 54]. In a bright place, it makes the pupil small so too much light does not get in[cite: 54].

Inside your eye, the light lands on a special spot at the back[cite: 54]. Nerves carry the message to your brain, and your brain makes the picture[cite: 54]. Your eyes are very soft and important, so your body protects them[cite: 54]. Eyelids close to keep dust out[cite: 54]. Eyelashes catch tiny bits of dirt[cite: 54]. Tears wash the eye clean and keep it wet[cite: 54].

🧪 ACTIVITY: Watch a Pupil Change

1. Sit with a grown-up in a dim room. Look at your own eyes in a mirror. Notice how big your pupils are[cite: 54].
2. Have the grown-up switch on the room light while you keep looking in the mirror. Watch your pupils get smaller![cite: 54]
3. Turn the light off again and wait. Do they get bigger?[cite: 54]

⚠️ Never shine a torch into anyone's eyes[cite: 52, 54].

🔍 Parts of the ear diagram ↗ 🔍 How sound travels into the ear ↗
Listening to Sounds Around Us
Music Makes Vibrations

You hear with your ears[cite: 55]. Every sound is made by something vibrating, which means wiggling back and forth very fast[cite: 55]. A guitar string wiggles[cite: 55]. A drum skin wiggles[cite: 55]. Your voice box wiggles when you talk[cite: 55]. These wiggles push the air, and the air carries the wiggles to your ears[cite: 55].

The part of the ear you can see is like a funnel that catches sounds[cite: 55]. Inside, the wiggles hit a thin skin called the eardrum[cite: 55]. The eardrum wiggles like the top of a little drum[cite: 55]. Next, tiny bones and a snail-shaped part inside your head turn the wiggles into messages[cite: 55]. Nerves carry the messages to your brain, and your brain says, "That is a dog barking!"[cite: 55]

💡 FUN FACT: Loud sounds have big wiggles that can hurt the tiny parts inside your ear[cite: 55]. Keep music and headphones low, and cover your ears near very loud noises[cite: 55].

🧪 ACTIVITY: Listen and Feel

Feel the voice: Put your fingers gently on your throat and hum[cite: 55]. What do you feel? That is your voice vibrating![cite: 55]
Twang: Stretch a rubber band between your fingers and pluck it[cite: 55]. Can you see and hear it wiggle?[cite: 55]

🔍 How does the nose smell diagram ↗

You smell with your nose[cite: 56]. Everything that has a smell gives off tiny, tiny bits that are too small to see[cite: 56]. These bits float in the air[cite: 56]. When you breathe in, the bits go up your nose to a special patch high inside[cite: 56]. There, nerves pick up the message and send it to your brain[cite: 56]. Your brain says, "Yum, that is bread!" or "Yuck, that is rotten!"[cite: 56]

Your sense of smell helps you in many ways[cite: 56]. It helps you enjoy food and flowers[cite: 56]. It can also warn you of danger, like smoke from a fire or milk that has gone bad[cite: 56]. Smell and taste are best friends[cite: 56]. When you have a cold and your nose is blocked, food can seem to have very little taste[cite: 56].

💡 FUN FACT: A dog's nose is much, much better than ours[cite: 56]. Dogs can smell things people cannot smell at all, which is why some dogs are trained to find lost people[cite: 56].

Page 7: Taste — Your Tongue & Taste Test

Day 3

▢ SEE IT FIRST! Taste: Your Tongue

Open Google Images and search: taste buds on tongue for kids[cite: 57]
Then search: five basic tastes sweet sour salty bitter umami[cite: 57]
Look at the little bumps on the tongue. Those bumps hold your taste buds[cite: 57].

🔍 Taste buds on tongue ↗ 🔍 Five basic tastes ↗

You taste with your tongue[cite: 57]. Look at your tongue in the mirror[cite: 57]. It is covered with tiny bumps[cite: 57]. On the bumps are even tinier taste buds, and you have thousands of them![cite: 57] When food touches the taste buds, they send a message to your brain about what you are tasting[cite: 57].

Your tongue can tell five main tastes: sweet (like honey), sour (like lemon), salty (like chips), bitter (like dark chocolate), and umami, which means savory, like the taste of soup or cheese[cite: 57]. Some people think that each taste happens on only one part of the tongue. That is a myth! Every part of your tongue can taste all of them[cite: 57, 65].

Taste works together with smell[cite: 57]. Try this: pinch your nose shut, close your eyes, and have a grown-up give you a small piece of apple and a small piece of raw potato[cite: 57]. Can you tell which is which? Now let go of your nose and try again[cite: 57]. Your nose helps your tongue a lot![cite: 57]

Page 8: Touch — Your Skin & Mystery Bag

Day 4

▢ SEE IT FIRST! Touch: Your Skin

Open Google Images and search: layers of skin diagram for kids[cite: 58]
Then search: braille alphabet for kids to see how some people read with their fingertips[cite: 58].

🔍 Layers of skin diagram ↗ 🔍 Braille alphabet ↗

You feel things with your skin[cite: 58]. Skin covers your whole body, and it is your body's biggest organ![cite: 58] Inside your skin are millions of tiny sensors[cite: 58]. They pick up messages about what things feel like: hot or cold, soft or rough, smooth or bumpy, light touches and hard pressure[cite: 58]. The sensors send the messages along nerves to the brain[cite: 58].

Some parts of your skin are extra good at feeling[cite: 58]. Your fingertips and lips have lots of sensors, so they can feel very small details[cite: 58]. Skin also feels pain[cite: 58]. Pain is not fun, but it is very useful[cite: 58]. It tells you to pull your hand away from something hot or sharp before you get badly hurt[cite: 58, 65].

💡 FUN FACT: Some people cannot see, so they read with their fingertips using a special code of raised dots called braille[cite: 58]. Their sense of touch is very strong![cite: 58]

Page 10: Safety — Senses Keep Us Safe

Day 4

▢ SEE IT FIRST!

Open Google Images and search: how the senses keep us safe for kids[cite: 60]

🔍 How senses keep us safe ↗

Your senses are like a team of guards[cite: 60]. They watch for danger all day long[cite: 60]. Eyes spot a car coming[cite: 60]. Ears hear a horn or an alarm[cite: 60]. Your nose smells smoke[cite: 60]. Your tongue tastes when food has gone bad[cite: 60]. Your skin feels that a pan is too hot[cite: 60]. The sense organs send the message, the nerves carry it, and the brain decides what to do, sometimes in a split second![cite: 60]

Think and write. Finish each sentence with a sense and a body part[cite: 60].

1. If I smell smoke, my tells me to tell a grown-up right away[cite: 60].

2. If I hear a loud honk, my tell me to stop and look[cite: 60].

3. If a cup feels very hot, my tells me to pull my hand away[cite: 60].

4. If milk tastes sour and bad, my tells me not to drink it[cite: 60].

5. If I see a red light, my tell me to stop[cite: 60].
