📖 Section 1: Lesson Notes & Key Concepts

Part A: How Our Senses Work (Sensory Receptors)

Your body collects information about the world using specialized cells called Sensory Receptors. These receptors detect stimulus (energy changes) in your environment and send electrical signals along neural pathways directly to your brain.

Photoreceptors

Located in the eyes. They detect light energy and allow you to perceive color, movement, and shapes.

Mechanoreceptors

Located in skin and inner ears. They detect physical pressure, sound vibrations, touch, and balance.

Chemoreceptors

Located in the nose and tongue. They detect chemical molecules in air (smell) and food (taste).

Part B: Living vs. Non-Living (MRS GREN)

To decide if something is scientifically "living," biologists check if it carries out all seven life processes. We remember them using the acronym MRS GREN:

M - Movement
R - Respiration
S - Sensitivity
G - Growth
R - Reproduction
E - Excretion
N - Nutrition
